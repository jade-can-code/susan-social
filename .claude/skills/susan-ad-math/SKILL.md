# Ad Math

Work only from numbers the owner gives you. Show every formula and input so they can check it. Never fill a gap with a guess; ask for the missing number or show a range clearly labeled as an assumption.

## Core formulas

| Measure | Formula |
|---|---|
| Cost per click (CPC) | spend ÷ clicks |
| Click-through rate (CTR) | clicks ÷ impressions |
| Conversion rate (CVR) | results ÷ clicks (or ÷ landing page views) |
| Cost per lead (CPL) | spend ÷ leads |
| Cost per sale / acquisition (CPA) | spend ÷ sales |
| Return on ad spend (ROAS) | revenue from ads ÷ ad spend |
| Lead-to-sale rate | sales ÷ leads |
| Average order value (AOV) | revenue ÷ orders |
| Lifetime value (LTV) | average profit per customer over their whole time as a customer |
| Marketing efficiency (MER) | total revenue ÷ total marketing spend |

## Targets that protect profit

- **Break-even CPA** = profit per sale (price minus delivery costs, fees, refunds).
- **Target CPA** = break-even CPA × the share of profit you're willing to spend (often 30 to 50% for a new offer).
- **Max CPL** = target CPA × lead-to-sale rate.
  Example: R2,000 profit per sale, willing to spend 40% → target CPA R800. If 1 in 10 leads buys, max CPL = R80.
- **Break-even ROAS** = 1 ÷ profit margin.
- With repeat purchases or upsells, the owner may accept a higher CPA. Base that on real LTV, not hope.

## Budget forecast

Leads needed = sales goal ÷ lead-to-sale rate. Budget = leads needed × expected CPL. Show low, likely and high cases using the owner's own past numbers.

## Reading results

- Too few results to judge? Say so. Small numbers swing wildly; wait for more data.
- Compare like with like (same dates, same objective).
- Look at the whole chain: a cheap lead that never buys is expensive.

## Output

A short table of inputs, results and targets, then two or three plain sentences: what it means and the one decision it points to.
